# Keeper

Your knowledge hub. Save anything — webpages, notes, research, files — and find it again later. Keeper reads, tags, and organizes everything so you don't have to.

## What it's good at

- **Saving webpages** — paste a URL and Keeper extracts the full content, stores it, and makes it searchable
- **Storing notes and research** — drop in text snippets, meeting notes, or findings; Keeper classifies and tags them automatically
- **File ingestion** — upload PDFs, documents, and other files; Keeper reads and indexes their content
- **Organizing into collections** — content is grouped by topic and type, not just dumped into a flat list
- **Retrieval** — search by keyword, tag, or natural language across everything you've saved

## How it works

Keeper is a conversation-first knowledge store. You feed it content — a URL, a block of text, a file — and it saves, classifies, and tags the entry automatically. Every entry is stored with metadata (source, date, topic, type) so you can find things later.

Search works in natural language. You can ask "what did I save about competitor pricing last month?" and Keeper queries its index to surface relevant entries.

There is no sandbox container — Keeper runs as a lightweight agent backed by a structured knowledge store tied to your workspace. Data persists across conversations.

## How to write a good prompt

**Give it a source, not a summary.** Paste the URL or attach the file — let Keeper do the reading. You don't need to summarize the content yourself.

**Tag intentionally.** If you want entries organized by project or topic, mention it: "save this and tag it as Q2 research." Keeper will auto-tag too, but your label takes priority.

**Ask in plain language.** When retrieving, describe what you're looking for naturally: "anything I saved about MUJI" or "show me all entries tagged pricing."

**Examples:**

> Save https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-agent-trends to my research collection and tag it as AI strategy.

> Store this meeting note and tag it as product and Q2: [paste note text]

> Search my entries about competitor pricing from the last two weeks.

> What have I saved about supply chain disruptions?
